## 4. Backtests

Four tests, each of which can fail on its own.

- **Kupiec** (`kupiec_p`): is the breach rate right? Rejected when the observed rate is
  inconsistent with the level, in *either* direction - a model with no breaches is mispriced,
  not conservative.
- **Christoffersen** (`ind_p`): are breaches independent? A model that breaches in runs has
  the right average and is still useless, because the breaches arrive together.
- **Conditional coverage** (`cc_p`): the two above, jointly.
- **Engle-Manganelli** (`dq_p`): is the breach predictable from its own lags or from the VaR?
  Strictly stronger than the other two; it can detect a misspecification they both miss.

## 6. Multi-day scaling

The square-root-of-time rule says an h-day variance is h times a one-day variance. That is
true for i.i.d. returns and false for GARCH returns, whose variance is random and
mean-reverting. Simulating the recursion gives the ratio directly.

- Mean deviation of the simulated 10-day VaR from the scaled one-day VaR: **3.02%**
- Share of days on which the scaling rule understates the simulated risk: **77.8%**

Negative values are the aftermath of a shock: volatility is high and expected to fall, so
scaling by the square root of time is too cautious. Positive values are quiet periods, where
the same rule assumes volatility stays put and it does not. Both directions appear, which is
why the rule is a statement about the regime as much as about the horizon.
